Coin Toss Simulator: Learning Probability Through Experimentation

Introduction

Probability is the foundation of decision-making under uncertainty. Managers use probability when forecasting demand, evaluating risk, predicting customer behavior, and making investment decisions.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand random experiments.
  • Identify sample space and outcomes.
  • Calculate theoretical probability.
  • Calculate experimental probability.
  • Understand the Law of Large Numbers.
  • Interpret probability through charts.

Key Concepts

  • Random Experiment: An action with uncertain outcome.
  • Sample Space: {H, T}
  • Theoretical Probability: P(H)=0.5, P(T)=0.5
  • Experimental Probability: Based on observed outcomes.
  • Law of Large Numbers: Experimental probability approaches theoretical probability as trials increase.

Educational Impact

This simulator enables experiential learning by allowing students to perform repeated experiments and observe randomness in real time. Students compare theoretical and experimental probability while interpreting visual evidence through charts and graphs.

Reflection Questions

  1. Does Head occur exactly 50% of the time?
  2. What happens after 100 tosses?
  3. Why does the probability curve move toward 0.5?
  4. How is experimental probability different from theoretical probability?
